## Use the results to start a review

The scorecard helps turn a broad concern about your design system into a more specific conversation. Complete it with someone who maintains the library and someone who uses it. Where your answers differ, look at a real component, a recent release, or a support request together.

The five subjects cover how components behave, how design decisions reach code, and how people find and maintain the system. A strong total can still hide a problem in one subject, so read the breakdown before choosing what to tackle.

## Check the evidence behind an answer

This is a self-assessment. It does not inspect your repository, test accessibility, or measure adoption. The recommendations follow your answers, so treat them as prompts to investigate rather than proof of system quality.

Choose a specific gap and verify it in a product that uses the system. For example, if keyboard behavior is uncertain, test a complete workflow rather than an isolated component. If documentation scores poorly, watch an engineer try to use an unfamiliar component.

## Share a snapshot

Your completed report is encoded in its URL. Save the link to revisit it, or share it with a teammate. Anyone with that link can read the results; no account is needed.
