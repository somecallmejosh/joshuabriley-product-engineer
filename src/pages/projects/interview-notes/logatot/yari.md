- **Participant:** Yari
- **Role:** Spanish-speaking home-based childcare provider
- **Interview format:** One-on-one conversation with Spanish language support
- **Primary topics:** Documentation, reporting, language access, after-hours administrative work, and funding

**Interviewer:** Thank you for speaking with me today, Yari. I am researching what it is like to manage a home-based childcare program.

I am especially interested in daily recordkeeping, reporting, licensing, reimbursements, and the administrative work you complete after the children leave.

We can conduct the interview in Spanish, and your responses can be translated into English for the research.

**Yari:** Yes, that would be much easier for me. I understand some English, but when the documents use formal or legal words, it becomes difficult.

**Interviewer:** To start, can you tell me about your childcare program?

**Yari:** I have provided childcare from my home for almost eight years.

I normally care for five or six children. Most are toddlers or preschool age. Sometimes I also care for an older child before or after school.

Most of the families speak Spanish at home. Some of the children are learning both English and Spanish.

**Interviewer:** Do you manage the program by yourself?

**Yari:** Most of the time, yes.

My sister sometimes helps me, but she also has another job. My husband may help with cleaning or moving equipment, but he does not help with the childcare records.

The responsibility is mine.

**Interviewer:** What does a typical day look like?

**Yari:** The first child comes before 7:00 in the morning.

I make breakfast, help the children arrive, talk with the parents, and make sure everyone has what they need.

During the day, we have meals, snacks, activities, outside time, naps, diaper changes, and bathroom breaks.

I also clean continuously. With young children, there is always something to clean.

The last child may leave around 5:30 or 6:00.

**Interviewer:** What happens after the last child leaves?

**Yari:** I clean the rooms, wash dishes, prepare some things for the next day, and then I look at the paperwork.

Sometimes I still need to enter attendance, meals, naps, or other information.

I may also need to answer parent messages, check payments, or review a letter from an agency.

By that time, I am very tired.

**Interviewer:** How much administrative work do you complete after hours?

**Yari:** Usually at least one hour, but sometimes more.

When I have a report due, I may work until 9:00 or 10:00 at night.

Sometimes I wait until the weekend because I do not have the energy during the week.

Then the paperwork begins to accumulate.

**Interviewer:** How does that affect you?

**Yari:** It is stressful because I feel that I am never finished.

When the children leave, the childcare work is finished, but the business work is not finished.

I still have my own family, dinner, laundry, and things in my house.

Sometimes I sit down to complete the records and fall asleep.

**Interviewer:** How do you record when children arrive and leave?

**Yari:** I have a paper attendance sheet near the entrance.

Parents are supposed to write the time and sign it. Some remember, but some forget.

I also enter attendance into an application.

**Interviewer:** Why do you use both paper and an application?

**Yari:** The paper has the parent’s signature. I may need that as proof.

The application helps me organize the information and communicate with parents.

But it means I sometimes enter the same information twice.

**Interviewer:** What happens when a parent forgets to sign?

**Yari:** I try to remember the time.

Sometimes I send a message asking the parent to confirm when they arrived or left.

This is difficult because parents are working too. They may not answer until later.

Then I have an empty space on the form, and I worry that someone will think I did not keep the correct records.

**Interviewer:** What else do you document during the day?

**Yari:** Meals, snacks, naps, diaper changes, bathroom use, injuries, medication, and sometimes activities.

For the food program, I have to show which children were present and which meals they received.

Parents also want to know what their child ate and whether they slept.

**Interviewer:** Are you able to enter that information as it happens?

**Yari:** Not always.

When I have several small children, I cannot stop to use the phone every time something happens.

If I am changing one child, another child may need help. If I am serving lunch, I am making sure everyone is sitting safely and eating.

Sometimes I make notes on paper. Sometimes I tell myself I will remember.

Later, I may not remember the exact time.

**Interviewer:** Can you describe a recent situation where that happened?

**Yari:** One child fell asleep earlier than normal. I was helping another child who was upset, so I did not enter the nap time.

At the end of the day, I remembered that the child slept, but I could not remember whether it was 12:15 or 12:30.

For a parent, that difference may not be important. But when I am completing a record, I feel like I should know the exact time.

**Interviewer:** Which documentation is most difficult for you?

**Yari:** The documents from the government or licensing agency.

The daily information is not always easy, but I understand it.

The difficult part is when I receive a letter, a new form, or instructions that are only in English.

Sometimes the translation is available, but not always.

Sometimes the form is in Spanish, but the website or instructions are in English.

**Interviewer:** What do you do when you receive a document you do not understand?

**Yari:** First, I try to translate it on my phone.

Sometimes I ask my daughter. She speaks English well, but I do not like asking her to help with business documents.

I may ask another provider or call the agency.

The problem is that when I call, it may be difficult to reach someone who speaks Spanish.

**Interviewer:** What happens when an interpreter is available?

**Yari:** It helps, but it can take longer.

Sometimes I call one number, wait, and then they connect an interpreter.

Then I explain the situation to the interpreter, and the interpreter explains it to the other person.

If the question is complicated, I am not always sure that everything was understood correctly.

**Interviewer:** Are translated documents usually easy to understand?

**Yari:** Not always.

Some translations use words that people do not use in normal conversation.

The document may be technically translated, but it is still difficult.

Sometimes I understand every word but still do not understand what I am supposed to do.

**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example?

**Yari:** I received a notice about updating information for a child receiving assistance.

I understood that something had changed, but I did not understand whether I needed to complete a new form or whether the parent needed to contact the agency.

I called the parent. The parent did not know either.

I spent several days trying to find the answer because I was afraid the payment would stop.

**Interviewer:** Did the issue affect your payment?

**Yari:** The payment was delayed.

I eventually received the money, but I did not know when it would come.

I had already provided the childcare. I had already purchased food and supplies.

That is very stressful for a small provider.

**Interviewer:** How do you keep track of reporting deadlines?

**Yari:** I write them on a paper calendar and put reminders on my phone.

Sometimes I receive an email. Sometimes I receive a paper letter.

The information comes from different places.

I worry that I will miss something because I did not see the email or did not understand that the letter required an action.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever missed a deadline?

**Yari:** I submitted something late once because I misunderstood the date.

The form showed one date, but there was another date in the instructions.

I thought the first date was the deadline.

The agency accepted the form, but I was very worried.

**Interviewer:** What did you learn from that experience?

**Yari:** Now I call when I am not sure.

But calling takes time. I may have to call during business hours when I am caring for children.

I cannot stay on the phone for 30 or 40 minutes when I am responsible for young children.

**Interviewer:** Is that one reason the work happens after hours?

**Yari:** Yes.

During the day, my first responsibility is the children.

I may see an email or receive a message, but I cannot always stop to read it carefully.

I save it for later.

After the children leave, I have many things waiting for me.

**Interviewer:** Do you participate in a food reimbursement program?

**Yari:** Yes.

The food program is helpful because food is expensive, especially when you want to provide healthy meals.

But the reporting requires a lot of attention.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through how you report meals.

**Yari:** I plan the menu and record breakfast, lunch, and snacks.

I need to document which children received each meal.

I also have to make sure the food includes the correct parts, like milk, fruit, vegetables, grains, or protein.

The requirements depend on the meal and sometimes on the child’s age.

**Interviewer:** Are the food program materials available in Spanish?

**Yari:** Some are.

I have received Spanish guides, but sometimes updates come in English first.

The online system is mostly English.

I know the steps because I have used it for several years, but when something changes, I may not understand immediately.

**Interviewer:** What happens if a meal record is incomplete?

**Yari:** I may not receive reimbursement for that meal.

Sometimes the sponsor contacts me and asks a question.

Other times, the meal is removed from the claim.

**Interviewer:** How easy is it to understand why a meal was removed?

**Yari:** Sometimes it is clear. Sometimes I see a code or a message that I do not understand.

Then I need to call or send an email.

It is frustrating because the food was served. The children ate the meal. But a problem with the documentation means I do not receive the money.

**Interviewer:** How much time does it take to complete the food claim?

**Yari:** If my daily records are complete, maybe one or two hours.

If I am behind, it can take much longer.

I have to compare attendance with the meals. I look at my notes and messages to remember whether a child was absent.

Sometimes I check photos on my phone to help me remember what happened on a certain day.

**Interviewer:** You use photos to reconstruct the records?

**Yari:** Sometimes.

For example, I may see that a child was in a picture during lunch. That helps me remember that the child was present.

I do not want to depend on that, but when the paperwork is behind, I use whatever information I have.

**Interviewer:** What causes the documentation to fall behind?

**Yari:** Tiredness is the biggest reason.

I know the records are important. I am not ignoring them.

But after caring for children for 10 or 11 hours, cooking, cleaning, and talking to parents, my brain is tired.

The forms require attention. If I am too tired, I may make more mistakes.

Sometimes I decide it is safer to wait until the weekend.

**Interviewer:** What happens on the weekend?

**Yari:** I may spend several hours completing records.

I also prepare activities, plan meals, shop for food, and clean.

It feels like I work seven days a week.

**Interviewer:** How does this affect your family?

**Yari:** My family understands, but it takes time away from them.

Sometimes my husband wants to go somewhere, and I say I need to finish the childcare paperwork.

Sometimes I am physically present with my family, but I am thinking about a report or a payment.

**Interviewer:** Do you feel that the administrative requirements are clearly explained?

**Yari:** No.

Some people explain things very well. But the whole system is not simple.

There are different rules for licensing, food reimbursement, childcare assistance, taxes, and grants.

Each program has its own website, username, password, forms, and deadlines.

**Interviewer:** Do those systems share information?

**Yari:** No, not from what I can see.

I enter the child’s name and attendance in different places.

I enter my address, license number, and program information again and again.

I do not understand why the systems cannot share basic information.

**Interviewer:** What childcare management tools do you currently use?

**Yari:** I use an application for parent communication, attendance, and payments.

I use a different website for the food program.

I use paper for signatures and some licensing forms.

I also have folders on my computer, but I am not always sure where I saved something.

**Interviewer:** What works well in the childcare application?

**Yari:** Messages and pictures work well.

Most parents can use the application in Spanish, which helps.

Payment reminders are also useful.

**Interviewer:** What does not work well?

**Yari:** There are too many screens.

Sometimes I want to record one simple thing, but I have to open the child, choose the day, choose the activity, enter the time, and save it.

When you repeat that for five or six children, it takes too long.

**Interviewer:** Does the application translate everything into Spanish?

**Yari:** Most of the main parts are translated.

But sometimes an error message or help page appears only in English.

Those are the moments when I need the Spanish the most.

When everything is working, I already know what to do. When there is a problem, the explanation is in English.

**Interviewer:** That sounds like an important distinction.

**Yari:** Yes. The Spanish should not only be on the easy screens.

It should also be available when there is a problem, a warning, a denied payment, or a change in the rules.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever stopped using a tool because it was too difficult?

**Yari:** Yes.

I tried one application that had many features, but the Spanish translation was poor.

Some words were translated incorrectly. Other parts were still in English.

Parents were also confused, so I stopped using it.

**Interviewer:** What would make a system easier for you?

**Yari:** Everything should be available in Spanish, including instructions and support.

The language should be simple.

I would like short explanations that tell me exactly what I need to do.

For example, instead of showing a code, it should say, “This child was marked absent. Remove the meal or correct the attendance.”

**Interviewer:** Would reminders help?

**Yari:** Yes, especially if they are clear.

I would want reminders before something is due, not only after it is late.

I would also want the reminder to tell me what document is needed and where to submit it.

**Interviewer:** How would you prefer to receive reminders?

**Yari:** A message on my phone would be best.

Email is useful, but I receive many emails.

A phone notification in Spanish would be easier to notice.

**Interviewer:** Would voice entry be helpful during the day?

**Yari:** Very helpful, especially if I could speak Spanish.

I could say, “Mateo ate lunch,” or “Sofía started her nap at one.”

That would be faster than stopping to type.

**Interviewer:** Would you trust voice entry for official records?

**Yari:** I would want to review it.

Names can be difficult. A system may misunderstand a child’s name or a Spanish word.

It should show me what it heard and let me approve or correct it.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the original audio saved?

**Yari:** Maybe for a short time, in case there is a mistake.

But I would want to know who can hear it and how long it is saved.

There are private conversations in the childcare room. I would not want the system listening all day.

It should only listen when I press a button or give a command.

**Interviewer:** What would help you complete records before the end of the day?

**Yari:** A simple review screen.

Before the last child leaves, the system could show me what is missing.

Maybe it says, “Lunch is missing for two children,” or “One departure time is missing.”

Then I could correct it while I still remember the day.

**Interviewer:** What would you want to see at the beginning of the day?

**Yari:** Which children are coming, schedule changes, allergies, parent messages, and any documents that are almost expired.

I would also like to know whether a child’s assistance authorization is ending soon.

**Interviewer:** What would you want at the end of the day?

**Yari:** I would want the system to say that everything is complete.

I want to close the workday and know that I do not have two more hours of paperwork waiting for me.

**Interviewer:** How do licensing visits make you feel?

**Yari:** Nervous.

I know I take good care of the children.

But I worry that I misunderstood a form or missed a signature.

When English is not your first language, you are always wondering whether you understood the rule correctly.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever had a finding because of documentation?

**Yari:** I had a form that was missing a parent signature.

The information was complete, but the signature line was empty.

It was corrected, but I felt embarrassed.

The inspector was not rude, but I felt like I had failed.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel the finding reflected the quality of care you provided?

**Yari:** No.

The child was safe. I had the emergency information and everything I needed.

But the form was not complete.

I understand why records are required, but sometimes the paperwork makes good providers feel like they are not doing a good job.

**Interviewer:** Have language difficulties ever made you avoid applying for funding?

**Yari:** Yes.

I have seen grants that looked helpful, but the application was long and only in English.

I did not know how to answer some of the questions.

I was afraid I would write something incorrectly, so I did not apply.

**Interviewer:** What kind of funding would help your program?

**Yari:** Money for outdoor equipment, learning materials, home repairs, and technology.

I would also like funding to hire someone for a few hours each week to help with administrative work.

**Interviewer:** Would administrative assistance be as valuable as physical equipment?

**Yari:** Yes, maybe more valuable.

Equipment helps the children, but time helps everything.

If someone helped me organize forms, enter information, or apply for grants, I would have more time and energy for the children and my family.

**Interviewer:** What is the biggest problem you face?

**Yari:** The biggest problem is that the paperwork continues after the childcare day is finished.

The systems are not designed for someone who has been caring for children all day.

They expect us to sit at a computer and complete everything as if that is our only job.

**Interviewer:** Which problem creates the most financial risk?

**Yari:** Missing or incorrect documentation.

If the attendance is wrong, the subsidy payment can be affected.

If the meal record is wrong, the food reimbursement can be affected.

If I miss a grant deadline because I did not understand the instructions, I lose another opportunity.

**Interviewer:** Which problem creates the most stress?

**Yari:** Not knowing whether I understood the information correctly.

When a document is only in English, I may read it many times.

I worry that I missed something important.

**Interviewer:** What would you solve first?

**Yari:** I would create one system that lets me record information quickly in Spanish and use that information for all the reports.

I should not have to translate the same information, enter it several times, and then worry that the records do not match.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything else people should understand about providers who primarily speak Spanish?

**Yari:** We are capable of running good childcare programs.

The problem is not that we cannot understand childcare or business.

The problem is that important information is not always provided in a language and format we can use.

A translated button is not enough.

We need the instructions, warnings, support, and official documents to be clear too.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything else you would like to add?

**Yari:** Providers need time.

Every new rule creates more work. Every new system promises to save time, but many of them create another place where we have to enter information.

I would like technology that helps me finish my work during the day so that, when the children leave, I can be finished too.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Yari. Your experience highlights how documentation, language access, and after-hours work are connected.

**Yari:** Thank you. I hope the research helps create something that providers can actually use.
